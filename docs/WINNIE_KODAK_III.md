# Winston "Winnie" Kodak III | The Forgotten Darkroom

**Universe:** TweedleVerse, Stemside  
**Home series/game:** TweedlePD (TVPD), with potential appearances in other Stemside stories  
**Document type:** Character canon + optional gameplay pitch, **not an implemented feature**  
**Original character concept:** Michael Wave / Backyard Production Company

> He has photographed every important moment in Stemside history. Finding the right picture is another matter.

## Canon boundaries

**Established by creator (LOCKED)**

- **Name:** Winston Kodak III; everyone at the department calls him **Winnie**.
- **Job:** TVPD's relentlessly intrusive police photographer. He enters scenes without warning, photographs the characters in the middle of the action, and offers a bewilderingly inappropriate menu of print options.
- **Hidden location:** Somewhere in the police department is a **secret room the department forgot existed**.
- **The room's defining visual:** **Miles of tiny photographic negatives**, hanging, looping, and stretching through the forgotten space. The strips themselves should be a major sight gag and a possible exploration mechanic.

**Working proposals (not locked)**

- His self-important stationery insists on "Winston Kodak III" while everyone, including the chief, calls him Winnie.
- Each appearance brings a different camera, from a battered pocket camera to an absurdly elaborate studio rig.
- The hidden room can connect to forgotten filing cabinets, maintenance passages, and a long-abandoned one-hour photo counter.
- Some strips depict old departmental mistakes, forgotten city events, or events that apparently have not occurred yet. The last possibility is an optional surreal gag, **not a required explanation or time-travel canon**.
- A pharmacy he relied on to develop prints closed decades ago; Winnie may not have noticed. Keep this mutually contradictory with his massive in-house negative archive if it makes the scene funnier.

## Character engine

**Winnie's want:** A perfectly composed, perfectly catalogued photograph and, preferably, an upsell.  
**Winnie's blind spot:** He has no instinct for when the camera, sales pitch, or lighting assistant is inappropriate.  
**Winnie's superpower:** Being in the frame a fraction of a second before anyone expects him.  
**Winnie's limitation:** He can document evidence, but cannot magically solve cases. An actual investigator still has to interpret it.

**Performance:** Calm, precise, professional, quietly pleased with a good exposure. He is never malicious. His timing is terrible and his commitment is absolute. Never play him as a copy of an existing film character or quote an existing movie.

**Recurring escalation:** Start with an unwanted photo. Later add an elaborate camera setup, backdrop, backdrop assistant, print-finishing kiosk, receipt printer, or optional portrait package at the most narratively inconvenient moment. On rare occasions he appears mid-boss confrontation. The joke remains character-first: Winnie thinks he is doing excellent work.

**Visual rules:** Original fictional silicon-person design, no real Kodak logo, signature product trade dress, or borrowed visual design. The *character's surname* is Kodak; the equipment and photo-lab branding must be invented for TweedleVerse.

## The room nobody remembers

**Working location name:** *The Forgotten Darkroom* (under/behind the TVPD precinct).

- The official precinct floor plan omits it. The door has been reclassified, relabeled, and built around for years.
- Thousands upon thousands of little negative strips descend from the ceiling like curtains, coil through shelving, disappear into vents, and return from somewhere implausibly far away.
- A tiny island of normalcy sits in the middle: Winnie's desk, a lunch bag, an ordinary desk lamp, and a mountain of unclaimed print envelopes.
- The department's lost history survives on these strips. Most of it is trivial: a promotion ceremony, a misplaced donut, an argument about an office plant, or someone cutting a ribbon at a door that no longer exists.
- **The joke is that the archive is massive but not inherently sinister.** Interesting mysteries can emerge from real evidence without turning Winnie into a villain.

### Location reveal: a playable/animated scene pitch

The player follows an unclaimed photo receipt through the precinct basement, past signage that disagrees about what floor they are on. A door opens.

Instead of one darkroom, there are **miles** of negatives. As the camera pans, the strips keep going. Then Winnie casually steps out from behind a curtain of film, eating lunch.

**Chip:** "Winnie. What is all this?"

**Winnie:** "The department records."

**Chip:** "There's enough film here to wrap around the city."

**Winnie:** "Twice. If you don't include the Christmas parties."

**Chip:** "Nobody knew this room existed."

**Winnie:** "Then I suppose nobody will want the reprints."

The camera reveals a sign: **PICKUP IN ONE HOUR**. A number display reads **NOW SERVING 004**, permanently.

## Future optional TweedlePD case: "The Negative Space"

**Status:** Concept only. Does **not** introduce a playable room, quest, badge, map connection, or save key yet.

**Premise:** An old piece of evidence has gone missing from the precinct's official casebook. Winnie knows exactly where the photographs are. Unfortunately, knowing where *in a city of dangling film* is another matter.

**Suggested beats:**

1. **Uninvited portrait:** Winnie interrupts a genuine case briefing with an unnecessary group photo. An odd photo envelope provides a clue to the forgotten room.
2. **Find the door:** Check contradictory maintenance records and an out-of-order photo counter in TVPD. The puzzle must be solvable without knowing obscure real-world film-developing terminology.
3. **Follow the contact sheets:** Trace dates, scene details, and recurring visual motifs across film strips. Wrong selections produce funny but recoverable red herrings.
4. **The almost-reveal:** A photo seems to show a future incident. A normal camera angle, mislabeled date, or purposeful ambiguity can explain it; do not lock supernatural lore until a future story chooses to.
5. **Solve the actual case:** Use a *specific, corroborated* photo as evidence rather than treating the existence of a giant archive as proof. Winnie tries to sell the chief an enormous framed print.

**Possible reward:** A souvenir contact sheet, character dialogue unlock, or a non-game-changing "Department Historian (Unpaid)" title. These are suggestions only. Do not silently change the already-shipped 15 Passport stamps, Crossroads achievements, or existing badge definitions.

### Gameplay principles if implemented

- Introduce Winnie as a recognizable recurring NPC **before** revealing the room. The reveal should earn its surprise.
- Make film paths readable: labeled reels, optional clue highlights, a navigable diagram, keyboard/touch/gamepad-friendly controls, and a straightforward exit. A visually elaborate room must not become an unreadable maze.
- Do not use real camera flashes or aggressive screen strobing as a mandatory mechanic. Respect reduced-motion settings and provide a non-flashing visual cue for his shutter gag.
- Keep the experience nonviolent and fictional. Evidence choices should have legible feedback and no permanent softlocks.
- Keep save ownership isolated. Any new case must use its own versioned slot, read existing TweedlePD/Passport progress only through validated interfaces, and never overwrite the existing Case #001, Patrol Shift, RPG, or World Map data.
- No dependencies, hosted services, account requirements, or new third-party assets without separate approval.

## Other recurring appearances

- **Chief McBadge briefing:** Winnie builds an unnecessary portrait setup behind the shouting chief. No one mentions the backdrop.
- **Reggie's public appearance:** Reggie orders Winnie to crop out the crowd; Winnie presents an invoice for removing people.
- **Street encounter:** A police evidence photo accidentally becomes a promotional portrait of a completely unrelated passerby.
- **Cross-property cameo:** A background negative shows a place from another TweedleVerse game, creating an optional in-world connection rather than a required continuity retcon.

## One rule above all

**Play it straight.** The chief can yell, the walls can be lined with endless negatives, and the photo counter can ask about premium finishes. Winnie behaves as though this is a perfectly normal workday. The joke grows out of the contrast.
