# Merv and *The Last Exposure*

**Universe:** TweedleVerse / Stemside  
**Location anchor:** Winnie Kodak III's forgotten TVPD darkroom  
**Story status:** Creator-established premise with clearly separated design proposals; **not implemented gameplay**  
**Created for:** Michael Wave / Backyard Production Company

> One-way ticket. One photograph to send home. No blank film left.

## Canon boundary: what we have established

**Creator-established / LOCKED concept**

- **Merv** is a small, unusual, tentacle-like visitor **from another world**, with an original silhouette and personality.
- Merv was **sent to Stemside for reconnaissance** on a **one-way mission**. He was not promised a ride back.
- His spacecraft resembles an **old instant-photo camera**. Its official working name is **The Last Exposure**.
- His mission provided **a single photograph to transmit home containing all gathered intelligence**. One image must carry a mission's worth of information.
- **Merv has run out of blank photographs**, leaving the camera-ship stranded. The remaining availability of any *special* final-report sheet is unresolved: do not casually grant him a spare.
- His photos **can be more than pictures: some are portal-capable**, opening a passage or window into another place or world.
- Merv has become connected to **Winston "Winnie" Kodak III**, the TVPD photographer, whose secret forgotten darkroom is filled with miles of dangling negatives. Their friendship and co-archivist relationship are a central character direction.

**Proposed, not yet fixed**

- Merv's homeworld name, species, rank, agency, language, facial design, number of arms, and mission duration.
- Whether his reconnaissance target was a Spacealite signal leak, the whole city, or a phenomenon larger than Stemside.
- Whether a uniquely marked final report plate survives, is lost, or must be reconstructed. **The established scarcity is real; no free extra blanks.**
- Precisely which photos are portals and whether their destinations are physical locations, remembered exposures, or distant dimensions.
- Whether Winnie knows the full truth and simply chooses not to mention it, or has mistaken an alien spacecraft for antique photo equipment.

## 1. The ship: THE LAST EXPOSURE

The ship is a **palm- or desk-sized retro-futurist instant-camera spacecraft**, with a chunky geometric body, a single expressive lens like a cyclopean eye, fold-out landing supports, and a narrow ejection slot. Its paint and engineering should be entirely original: an echo of the **general instant-camera era**, not a reproduction of a specific commercial camera, logo, or patented product design.

**Suggested interaction cues**

- The lens dilates and focuses as the ship navigates; its shutter opens like a hatch.
- A launch sounds like a shutter motor winding up. The slot emits a little card that slowly develops into a destination.
- Its fuel indicator is not a battery gauge but **0 EXPOSURES REMAINING**.
- It tries to print a boarding pass when the ignition is turned. Nothing comes out.
- The cockpit can be larger on the inside, but that is optional rather than assumed canon.

**Operating idea (working rules):** The original voyage burned through limited blank dimensional-photo media to move between anchors. Once those are gone, the ship is grounded. Conventional Stemside negatives and ordinary photographic paper do **not** magically substitute for mission-grade portal stock.

**Important:** "One-way mission" is not a hidden promise of a return flight. Sending the report, if Merv ever manages it, need not rescue him.

## 2. The single-photo mission

Merv's orders were simple and absurdly severe: **observe another world, compress the findings into ONE image, and send it back**. A mission dossier folded into a single exposure: what happened, who caused it, and where the route goes. The photograph is simultaneously an archive, a message, and potentially a dimensional address.

The mission came with limited photo stock. Merv used it up getting to Stemside, surviving, and trying to finish his research. The last thing he needs to complete the assignment is exactly what he no longer has: a usable, authorized way to make that final exposure.

**The story question:** If a one-way scout has spent years building a life here, is completing the assignment still the most important thing? Winnie cares about *Merv*, not the classified results. Merv may come to care more about the person who kept him company than the people who sent him away.

**Do not decide yet:** Whether the report can be sent, whether the mission's sponsor is benevolent, whether a transmission would expose Stemside, or whether Merv deliberately withholds intelligence. These are future story choices, not solved facts.

## 3. Photographs that become portals

**Established concept:** Some of Merv's special images can function as portals.  
**Rules below are proposed for consistent puzzles and stakes.**

| Property | Working rule | Why it helps |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Blank stock | Rare, mission-grade dimensional media; a finite resource | The archive cannot print infinite shortcuts |
| Exposure | Captures a valid place, anchor, or signal, not just any scribble | Discoverable logic for players |
| Development | Image needs a visible process before it activates | Creates tactile puzzle moments |
| Viewing mode | The print can show a scene as a window or reveal hidden evidence | Low-cost reconnaissance and jokes |
| Transit mode | A *qualified* photo may open a temporary doorway to its anchor | Exploration without unrestricted teleporting |
| Return path | Not automatically reversible; no guaranteed route home | Preserves the one-way mission |
| Failure | Bad exposure, wrong anchor, or damaged emulsion gives comic distortion or a safe failure | Fun without softlocking a player |

**Ordinary photos vs dimensional photos:** Most of Winnie's miles of negatives are just photos. A tiny subset could contain anomalies, cached reflections, or clues about rare dimensional stock. The darkroom **does not** secretly contain thousands of ready-made portals. That would flatten the central scarcity problem.

**Visual shorthand:** A normal negative shows tonal inversion. A dimensional negative subtly develops *depth* when held to a lightbox, and only a correctly framed image can become a threshold. Avoid flickering/flashing effects as required interactions; make all effects reduced-motion safe.

## 4. Winnie + Merv: the comedy partnership

**Winnie:** A man entirely convinced his portrait studio is a professional operation.  
**Merv:** A highly trained operative who knows the universe is far stranger than the precinct.

Their routines barely intersect, which is why the relationship works.

- Winnie thinks Merv has excellent instincts for lighting. Merv is actually measuring a breach between dimensions.
- Merv spends hours sorting a top-secret mission archive; Winnie writes **MISC.** on the box and sorts it under **M**.
- Winnie never forces Merv to discuss his past. The friendship works even when the mission fails.
- Merv can duck behind film strips before an overzealous officer sees him. His presence becomes a gentle anticipation scare before the punchline.
- Their shared workspace includes a ship disguised as an antique photo kiosk. No one notices it has landing legs.

### Sample original exchange: film shortage

**Chip:** "Winnie, is that a spaceship?"

**Winnie:** "It's a camera."

**Chip:** "WHY DOES IT HAVE AN ENGINE?"

**Winnie:** "Automatic advance."

**Chip:** "And the little guy?"

**Winnie:** "Merv. He runs the lab."

**Chip:** "Where's he from?"

**Winnie:** "Very far away. Never asked for an address."

*The ship lights up: NO EXPOSURES REMAINING.*

**Chip:** "We're trapped?"

**Winnie:** "Only if you need a picture."

## 5. Future story: THE LAST EXPOSURE

**Type:** Potential larger story arc following the proposed TweedlePD case *The Negative Space*. This is **not** a shipped episode, save format, quest, or promise of a release.

**Act I: The camera that should not fly**

A routine photo request leads to the hidden negatives. The player sees a moving shadow and remembers the childhood terror of being ambushed around an adventure-game corner. The culprit is Merv, struggling to carry a giant extension cable. Winnie casually introduces him.

**Act II: Development error**

The team realizes the ship's ejected images show places that shouldn't fit inside a print. They investigate a handful of anomalies using film strips, contact sheets, and a portable viewer. Original point-and-click style puzzles reveal that the camera is a stranded reconnaissance craft.

**Act III: The empty cartridge**

Merv explains the one-way assignment: one photograph to summarize his findings, and **no usable blank media left**. A final authenticated transmission might be possible only if a missing original sheet is recovered, a dead device repaired, or a rare substitute devised. Those are *alternate pitch paths*, not canon solutions.

**Act IV: A decision worth the last exposure**

The characters face the real conflict: what information should the sole report reveal, to whom, and at what cost to Stemside? The mission is not solved by conjuring a free round-trip ticket. Merv's connection to Winnie matters more than the spectacle.

**Possible ending gag:** Winnie offers a tasteful walnut frame for the completed interdimensional transmission. It turns out to be the exact size of an enormous portal.

## 6. Game-design notes for a future implementation

- **Genre touchstones:** Original comedic point-and-click exploration, inventory puzzles, conversations, timing, discoverable room secrets, multiple problem-solving routes; evoke the genre rather than lifting scenes, character likenesses, copyrighted dialogue, or assets from older adventure games.
- **Mechanics:** Inspect negatives; combine evidence; align transparent layers; interpret changing prints; use validated photographic anchors to view or enter temporary pocket spaces; optional witty interactions that reward curiosity.
- **Scarcity:** Limited portal interactions should be story-controlled, not an irreversible resource trap. A puzzle's essential answer must remain obtainable even if the player experiments.
- **Pacing:** Introduce Merv as a shadow/puzzle before explaining him; establish Winnie as comic relief before revealing their friendship; preserve the genuine emotional dilemma late in the arc.
- **Accessibility:** All visual photo clues need text alternatives; no mandatory timed chase, photo flash, strobe or fine-motor-only camera focus; controls and focus must support keyboard, touch, and gamepad as applicable.
- **Implementation isolation:** If added to TweedlePD, use an independently versioned save key and validated read-only connections to existing games. Never change historical Passport stamps, Crossroads completion, or existing saves implicitly.
- **Reality check:** No current executable game mechanic, vehicle, new map node, art asset, audio, or browser feature is added by this document.

## 7. Unanswered design decisions

1. What was Merv *really* assigned to observe, and who receives his report?
2. Is the last mission-grade sheet destroyed, hidden somewhere in Winnie's archive, or still in inaccessible storage?
3. Are Merv's portals doors to places, moments, or signal-based projections?
4. Does Winnie know the danger of sending the report?
5. What would Merv freely choose if his orders were lifted?
