# Practical Smokers | Series bible v0.1

**Series within TweedleVerse.** Adult-oriented surreal silicon comedy with original sketches, a workplace friend ensemble, fictional media and meta-storytelling.

## Logline

Four hopelessly underqualified silicon friends work at TweedCorp Co. in South Stemside, accidentally collide with an elite Tweedleball circuit, and repeatedly humiliate the interdimensional cable baron Reggie Bands III, often by pulling out the script and pointing to his own lines.

## Format

- Target: flexible **12–22-minute scripted episodes**, with 2–3 improv-flavored challenges, 1–2 short fake ads or TV inserts, and a throughline.
- Production may combine scripted dialogue and constrained model-generated improvisation, edited and approved by human producers. All “hidden camera” scenes use fictional / consenting participant characters, never covert filming of real people.
- Clear comic A-story and escalating B-story. A one-shot absurd sketch can interrupt only if the edit returns to the main character objective.
- Main character relationships have ongoing heart: these people like each other, even when they are idiots.

## Structural signature

1. **Cold open** with a small TweedCorp crisis or a Spacealite program break.
2. **Workplace or neighborhood problem** that the four amplify.
3. **Challenge or public consequence** with self-sabotage and improvised instructions.
4. **Fourth/fifth-wall escalation:** script, narrator, camera, credits, budget or channel switch participates.
5. **Payoff:** the gang avoids a grand ambition while solving an embarrassingly small one.
6. **Tweedsie tag:** occasional mischievous visual button, not mandatory in every episode.

## Season one emotional spine

Chip's desire: **one free year of CoffeeChucks**. The gang's desire: stay together, earn a living, survive corporate nonsense. Reggie's desire: keep the rich and poor in their assigned categories. When Chip wins, the triumph should belong to the neighborhood, not just the athlete.

## Two short original scene seeds

### A. “Where's the script?”
The crew discovers a printed script under the couch. It predicts the next line. They deliberately skip a line, only for a cue card to descend and chastise them for unauthorized improvisation. Tweedsie silently erases the cue card. Reggie receives the bill for erasing it.

### B. “Reggie Bands's entrance”
Reggie storms into a meeting with custom orchestral music. Spacealite invoices him for using the theme. He cancels the theme to save money; every subsequent entrance is accompanied by one sad kazoo note played by Tweedsie.

## Sister series pitch: Tweedle 420 [DEVELOPMENT CONCEPT]

**Tweedle 420: Protect. Serve. Forget Why.** is a separate proposed **TVPD mockumentary** following Chief McBadge, rookie Chip, Winnie, Merv and a department that cannot remember why it responded to a call. In a town where fictional Tweed culture includes the officers, public trespass might mean a neighbor napping on somebody's lawn; pastry misplacement means someone apologizing for eating an extra pastry. The officers escalate petty misunderstandings into solemn procedures. **Tweedsie is always somewhere in the background**, quietly strolling by with his oversized fictional spliff, sometimes meeting the documentary lens while the police miss him.

This is a proposed TV series, **not** a released program or a retcon of playable TweedlePD cases. Keep cases nonviolent, character-driven, restorative, original and separate from real-world policing advice. Full format, episode slate, cameo contract, and pilot: [Tweedle 420 show bible](TWEEDLE_420.md).

The hidden-background gag also creates a separate proposed discovery game: [Where's Tweedsie?](WHERES_TWEEDSIE.md).

## Style rules

- Broad slapstick, wordplay, over-literal corporate language, fake advertisements, physical text and UI, surreal transitions.
- Sarcasm can be sharp; cruelty toward poor communities is not the show's point.
- No borrowed film clips, TV images, logos, songs or substantial dialogue.
- **Lenny Smokins:** only original composition and words for any eventual “I'm Real High” cue.
- Rendered characters are fictional silicon people; no suggestion they are live unsuspecting bystanders.
