# TweedleVerse | World Bible v0.1

**Status:** Development canon. “Locked” means explicitly established in the originating concept sessions; “Proposed” means a promising working idea, not yet a binding decision.

## The promise

TweedleVerse is a fictional retro-futuristic silicon civilization where bureaucracies, class divisions, bad branding, predatory subscriptions and increasingly bizarre corporate schemes are treated with perfect seriousness. Silly things have consequences. Friendships and neighborhood loyalty are sincere. The camera and the script are props.

The world has an original identity: dysfunctional silicon culture, an absurd product called Tweed, a sport that turns athletic achievement into elaborate physical comedy, and a trickster who plays with narrative reality.

**Tone DNA, not material to copy:** workplace-friend chaos, surreal sketch logic, outrageous film-spoof fourth-wall violations, outrageous sports-commentary improvisation, and warm underdog community satire. Do not lift external dialogue, likenesses, music, visual designs or exact set pieces.

## The geography: Stemside [LOCKED]

Stemside is a factory town anchored by TweedCorp Co. (TCC). The physical north/south division is reinforced by housing, marketing, transit and snobbery. Everybody seems to buy from companies that lead back to the same handful of owners.

### South Stemside [LOCKED]

A worn, lively working-class district, mocked as the “Southside Stems” neighborhood. Its residents are resourceful, funny and mutually supportive, not inherently foolish or inferior. Its businesses are cheap, loud, endlessly inventive.

- **CoffeeChucks:** “It's Coffee, Chuck. Drink It.” A straightforward coffee joint. Same parent company's beans as CoffeeCharles, fraction of the pretension.
- **Reggie's Reggie:** Discount Tweed, sold as “Cheap in a Pinch!” Recurring joke: a bag seems to contain more stems and seeds than product.
- **Lackluster Video:** “Be Kind. Rewind. Whatever.” VHS and late fees, despite interdimensional streaming. Can rent episodes from the future.
- **Taco McBurger Bell:** Fast food whose menu is an argument between tacos and burgers.
- **Snublishers Clearing House:** Sweepstakes prize patrol with enormous checks and prizes containing trapdoor terms.
- **Southside Stems League:** Local amateur Tweedleball, improvised arena and makeshift facilities. Players often outperform the privileged northside stars.
- **TweedCorp works:** Central workplace and source of cross-town corporate misery.

### North Stemside [LOCKED]

The polished, wealthier hill district. Every service is repackaged as a lifestyle tier.

- **CoffeeCharles:** Northside version of CoffeeChucks. “For the Distinguished Caffeinated.” Its beverage sizes are proprietary status signals.
- **Whole Moods:** Self-important high-end grocery and luxury Tweed boutique; brands feelings and local organic aesthetics.
- **Biestros / Le Biestro:** Status-conscious restaurants serving microscopic portions and enormous descriptions.
- **Royal Tweedleball Club:** Snobbish, high-fee sports establishment.
- **North Stemside Invitational:** Premier tournament hosted on luxurious grounds as part of the TGA Pro Tour.
- **Spacealite executive facilities:** Most conspicuous architecture belongs to a company that charges people to watch themselves.

### The midpoint and beyond [PROPOSED]

Median Strip 42 is a tiny patch of land that the gang accidentally owns. City hall, a satellite uplink, defunct transit stations and sealed underground tunnels can become future districts. Keep the first playable map small enough to be hand-crafted.

### TVPD's forgotten darkroom [LOCKED premise; layout PROPOSED]

Within the Tweedle Police Department precinct is an unremembered room occupied by **Winston "Winnie" Kodak III**, TVPD's compulsively professional photographer. The department forgot the room existed; it contains **miles of small photographic negatives**. The visual joke and Winnie's recurring intrusion into police scenes are established. The room's exact entrance, map footprint, history, impossible photographs, and any interactive case are still proposals. See [Winnie Kodak III — The Forgotten Darkroom](WINNIE_KODAK_III.md) for continuity and future-game notes.

### The Last Exposure: Merv's stranded camera-ship [LOCKED concept]

Merv is an off-world reconnaissance visitor living in Winnie's forgotten darkroom. He came on a **one-way mission** in an **instant-camera-shaped craft** called **The Last Exposure**; he had to return **one photo** containing his findings, but he has **no blank photographs left**. Some unusual photos can open **portals**, making the photo medium more than an image. The exact method of sending the final report, available special stock, portal mechanics, and his homeworld remain undecided. Most of Winnie's extensive archive is ordinary film, not an unlimited portal supply. See [Merv and The Last Exposure](MERV_THE_LAST_EXPOSURE.md).

## Silicon people [LOCKED]

The residents are fictional silicon people, **not human actors**. Different forms, looks and character designs are permitted. They eat, work, joke, compete, form friendships, collect physical media and maintain absurd corporate institutions. Technology-flavored puns are seasoning, not every punchline.

## Fictional botany [LOCKED]

The plant is called **Tweed**; the knowingly absurd Latin names are *Tweedimus Silativa* and *Tweedimus Indicone*. Grade hierarchy: **Reggie**, **Mids**, **Loud**.

- **Reggie:** Cheap and stemmy. Reggie's Reggie is its worst-known product.
- **Mids:** Respectable, unremarkable and unglamorous.
- **Loud:** Premium product, hype, elaborate marketing.
- *Tweedimus Hybridius* and *Tweedimus Existentialis* are optional comic variants [PROPOSED].

Tweed is fictional satire, not real botanical science, medical information, or product marketing. Its effects can be absurdly digital: buffering mid-sentence, opening too many tabs, imagining one's screensaver has philosophical ambitions.

## Monopolies and economy

**TweedCorp Co. (TCC) [LOCKED]:** The four protagonists' employer. Managers preach productivity while creating conditions where nothing gets done. Everyone uses the company as a launchpad for questionable inventions, challenges and schemes.

**Spacealite™ [LOCKED]:** Interdimensional cable corporation: “You Have Infinite Choices. None Are Included.” Owns channels and increasingly claims it owns fictional realities. The corporation levies broadcast fees, fourth-wall fees and premium sound fees. Its furious face is **Reginald Von Bandwidth III**, better known as **Reggie Bands III**, **Reggie Bands**, and (to his horror) **Seeds McStems**.

**TGA Pro Tour [LOCKED]:** The professional Tweedleball circuit, culminating in the North Stemside Invitational / Grand Stem. Corporate teams compete. The marginalized Southside Stems League has a route to qualification, which the northside establishment deeply resents.

**Spacealite channel 420 [PROPOSED]:** A programmable excuse for original interdimensional show fragments, trailers, sports commentary and fake advertising. No licensed TV footage is used.

## Humor and the reality contract

1. **The characters are sincere.** Everything can be ridiculous except the emotional honesty of their friendships.
2. **The fourth wall is rentable.** Characters talk to audiences and interact with captions, credits, camera crews, sound and edit points.
3. **The fifth wall is worse.** They can read scripts, argue with the show's engine, change scenes, enter a prewritten commercial, dispute the licensing budget, or discover who authored their dialogue.
4. **Breaks must have a payoff.** Do not use meta gags as an excuse for a plot without stakes.
5. **Punch up.** Land the joke on monopolists, snobs and systems rather than treating poverty as a deficiency.
6. **Tweedsie has trickster privileges.** He can cut through production boundaries; he is a mischievous protector, not a cruel tormentor.
7. **Running gags evolve.** Reggie grows more furious at his nickname. Coffee's price signals deepen. Corporate “improvements” make things worse.
8. **Keep continuity light but useful.** Record who knows what, where people live, which characters have met, and which joke has already paid off.

## Cross-property narrative rule

A single place, outfit, character trait, and invented brand should remain recognizable between show, game, minisodes and promotional snippets. An episode may play fast and loose with time, but reusable assets and stable character motivations make the multiverse coherent.
