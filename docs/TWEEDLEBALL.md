# Tweedleball | TGA sport prototype v0.1

**What it is:** A fictional signature sport for TweedleVerse and a possible **real-world safe, portable skill game**. Golf/disc golf target precision, foot juggling, badminton-style rallies, racquetball bank shots, bowling pins, and televised obstacle course spectacle. Prototype rules are not yet validated by playtest.

## Core sport: Tweedleball [PROPOSED RULESET]

The **Tweedle** is a soft, padded, lightweight throwable/kickable target object. A round might use a soft ring-disc or foam ball after testing flight, bounce, impact and easy handling. The playable shape is **not locked**.

- Teams: singles or doubles; recreational default doubles.
- Arena: roughly 12 m × 6 m prototype rectangle, with low divider/net, safe padded bank boards, ground scoring cups or wide target rings, and lightweight knockdown pins. Scale down for available space.
- Launch: serve the Tweedle from a marked zone toward the opposing zone.
- Allowed contacts after serve: foot, knee or broad-faced paddle. Up to three touches per team before return; never strike toward a person's face.
- Targets: knock over opponent's soft pins or land in a wide target.
- Scoring (first test): 1 per pin, 3 for target, +1 for legal bank shot, +1 for two controlled foot taps in the same rally. Avoid runaway bonus stacking; test 21-point games.
- An out-of-bounds or unsafe contact ends the rally. Reset pins and field after each scoring rally.
- Win at 21, two-point lead, cap 25 for predictable game length.

**Engineering note:** This version is a *first test design*, not a settled sport. A disc can behave poorly under both kick and paddle contact; playtests should determine whether to select one primary projectile or split into differentiated tournament modes.

## Gauntlet tournament format [PROPOSED]

The fictional TGA Pro Tour adds spectacular obstacle course stages:

1. **Stem Roller:** balance across rotating padded obstacles.
2. **Mids Masher:** dodge soft swinging props while carrying an inert ball.
3. **Flying Tweedle:** precision launches through increasing target sizes.
4. **Northside Wall:** climbing / vertical path in virtual arenas; real-world equivalents must be low-impact, supervised and fall-protected.
5. **Big Bowl:** oversized soft bowling pins.
6. **The Loud Round:** two finalists race to complete a target and obstacle sequence while the Wheel of Tweed changes rules.

The show exaggerates scale, sound, physics and chaos. **Real-world editions require independent hazard review, soft props, stable surfaces, accessibility modes, no uncontrolled impacts or dangerous heights, and no intoxication requirement.**

### Wheel of Tweed

Potential virtual modifiers: Double Points; Reverse the Course; Foam Hammer Time; Ball Swap; Sudden Death; Everybody Freezes; Release the Stempeders; Reggie's Rule Change. In a real match, exclude any modifier that creates surprise hazards.

## League structure [LOCKED concept, working details]

- **Southside Stems League:** scrappy local qualifiers, disliked by elite clubs.
- **Corporate Clash:** qualification route for a TweedCorp employee to represent TCC.
- **North Stemside Invitational:** prestige championship of the rich district.
- **Grand Stem:** culminating trophy event of the TGA Pro Tour.

Possible divisions: Reggie (amateur), Mids (intermediate), Loud (elite). Final labels subject to review.

## Story payoff

A TweedCorp worker accidentally becomes the Southside champion while trying to win a year's free CoffeeChucks; his victory unlocks a slot in the hostile North Stemside Invitational. Reggie Bands tries to alter the rules, but is thwarted by his own signatures.

## Video-game adaptation

Third-person obstacle skill game with readable timers, physics-based but fair soft-ball mechanics, paddle/foot inputs, checkpoint resets, two-player challenge modes, spectator commentary, replay capture and Tweedsie Easter eggs. Browser first, VR later as an option rather than a requirement.

## Playtest gates

Validate core projectile behavior; 3 touch rule; scoring clarity; time per round; safe course spacing; spectator readability; controller and keyboard mappings; accessibility; anti-frustration checkpoint design. Keep fictional sport and real-life safety standards distinct.
