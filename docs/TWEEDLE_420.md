> The emergency is real. The paperwork is made up. Nobody remembers the call.

## Creator-established premise [LOCKED concept]

- **Stemside is a Tweed town, and TVPD's officers use fictional Tweed too.** That cultural detail belongs to the comedy, not just the civilian cast. Do not conflate it with real-world law enforcement, driving safety, or actual drug effects.
- The show is an **original mockumentary following the absurdly ineffective TVPD**, with awkward documentary-camera pauses and characters playing mundane situations with immense seriousness.
- Police regularly arrive at a scene, dramatically announce an investigation, and then ask: **"Wait. Why are we here?"**
- Their "crimes" are usually tiny, nonviolent, fixable inconveniences dressed in imposing bureaucratic vocabulary: **"Public Trespass"** when a resident falls asleep on a neighbor's lawn; **"Pastry Misplacement"** when a hungry neighbor admits taking a pastry while munching out.
- **Tweedsie is always somewhere in the background** of the show: wandering by, casually smoking his conspicuously fictional spliff, unnoticed by the police. His silent visual cameos become a find-the-character game for viewers.
- Characters remain original silicon residents. The show is neither an imitation of a specific television series nor a commentary on real individual police officers.

### Running logic

The chief speaks as if every briefing is a citywide emergency. The department's evidence hierarchy is wildly out of proportion to the alleged offenses. The documentary crew notices contradictions, but isn't there to solve cases. **Most people in Stemside are neighbors, not villains.** Each case should ideally resolve with help, communication, restitution, or some hilariously unnecessary form, rather than punishment for harmless behavior.

**Tweed and duty:** The joke is the town's pervasive fictional culture, unhelpful bureaucracy and surreal distraction, not a practical recommendation to do safety-critical work while impaired. Where an officer would otherwise drive dangerously or mistreat someone, redirect the farce to parking, dispatch mix-ups, paperwork, or non-driving choices.

## Proposed pilot: "Scene of the Crime"

The TVPD descends on CoffeeChucks with a dramatic show of force. Nobody can name the call. Chief McBadge demands the perimeter be secured. Chip asks who summoned them. Dispatch calmly explains that **the chief called about his missing patrol car**. The documentary camera reveals it is parked directly outside.

*Chief:* "Then whose car am I sitting in?"

*Chip:* "Yours, Chief."

*Chief:* "SOMEONE OPEN AN INVESTIGATION."

Winnie enters and suggests a panoramic precinct portrait. Tweedsie walks through the background carrying snacks and silently looks into the camera.

**Comedy beat:** The smallest possible missing-item problem, the biggest possible press conference. No dangerous pursuit or frightening detention.

## The Tweedsie background contract

1. **Every planned episode contains an observable Tweedsie cameo**, not necessarily in the foreground: walking by, crossing a window, behind police tape, in a distant doorway, or reflected in a prop.
2. Tweedsie often **looks directly at the documentary lens**. The police never recognize the pattern.
3. Repeated views are encouraged: the cameo must be visually legible when paused or revisited, not rely on inaccessible single-frame flicker.
4. Escalate over a season. First a stroll; later repeat crossings in opposite directions, a fourth-wall nod, a ridiculous visual impossibility. Do not make him the cause of every case.
5. The eventual "same witness" investigation should be an earned payoff, with the chief unable to see Tweedsie even when he is standing immediately behind him.
6. A paused-screen spotting challenge can cross-promote the proposed [Where's Tweedsie?](WHERES_TWEEDSIE.md) game, but no mandatory app, account, tracking or paywall.

## Production and originality guardrails

- Original documentary framing, dialogue, silicon-person designs, locations, soundtrack and comedy beats. Classic improv cop comedies are **genre inspiration**, not material to reproduce.
- No copied TV footage, scripts, cast likenesses, recognizable original scenes, or unauthorized musical cues.
- Treat smoking as an adult-oriented fictional-world character trait; avoid real-world drug advice, underage targeting or suggesting impaired driving is safe.
- Nonviolent stakes and restorative resolutions are central. Satirize ridiculous bureaucracy and authority rather than vulnerable people.
- Scripted/fictional filming only: no secretly recording real bystanders.
- Cross-series connections to *Practical Smokers* and TweedlePD should remain consistent with shipped content.
