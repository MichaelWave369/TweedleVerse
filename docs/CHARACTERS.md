# Characters & comedy engines | v0.1

## Four protagonists: *working roster*, names not yet locked

These names were brainstormed during the concept session; maintain them as provisional until casting / character-design approval. Each character needs a strong desire and a specific way to make situations worse.

### Chip “Chronic” Chipwell [PROPOSED]
- TweedCorp quality assurance. A brilliant accidental athlete who avoids effort except to pursue a tiny personal reward.
- Motivations: friends, cheap food, **a year's free CoffeeChucks**.
- Signature behavior: doesn't notice his own improbable success.
- Season arc: wins a Southside Stems League qualifier and is compelled to represent the corporation at the North Stemside Invitational.
- Never reduce him to “the high guy”; the difference between laziness and resourcefulness drives his comedy.

### Dank-9000 [PROPOSED]
- TweedCorp sales / self-appointed manager of every opportunity.
- Motivations: status, sponsorship deals, appearing on TV and monetizing anyone else's triumph.
- Flaw: would sell a free cup of coffee back to the person drinking it.
- Season role: becomes Chip's promoter, to everyone's financial detriment.

### Buffer Bob [PROPOSED]
- TweedCorp compliance and rules. Uses a clipboard as emotional support.
- Motivations: order, legal clarity, keeping the gang employed.
- Flaw: follows obsolete regulations more faithfully than the living rules.
- Season role: uncovers the qualification loophole, then discovers Reggie's signature on it.

### Professor Terpenstein [PROPOSED]
- TweedCorp botanical engineering. Believes each failed experiment is a breakthrough.
- Motivations: invention, scientific respect, helping the crew in objectively questionable ways.
- Flaw: cannot distinguish a proof of concept from a final production release.
- Season role: designs Tweedleball equipment that works differently each match.

## The supporting antagonists and tricksters

### Reggie Bands III [LOCKED]
- Formal title/name: **Reginald Von Bandwidth III** [full expansion from brainstorm; confirm as final].
- Spacealite's imperious CEO. Considers basic cable a birthright, and other people's time a monetizable asset.
- Nicknames: Reggie Bands, Bands, Seeds McStems; all make him furious.
- Running gag: called the lowest grade of Tweed despite his expensive lifestyle. Even his personal theme music requires a licensing payment to his own company.
- Goal: keep corporate power and the privileged championship closed to South Stemside.
- Weakness: his own fine print. The gang regularly defeats him with documents he personally signed.

### Tweedsie [LOCKED]
- Mischievous gopher mascot and cross-property wildcard; species joke: *Tweedimus Gopheratus*.
- **Appearance:** soft green/brown fur, dreadlocks, sunglasses, gold chain with leaf pendant; a conspicuously oversized fictional spliff.
- **Personality:** super chill, playful, impossible to intimidate, secretly protective of everyday South Stemside residents.
- **Powers (comic logic):** appears in arbitrary frames, menus, store displays, credit sequences, and holes; changes or annotates episode scripts; walks through a painted door while others cannot.
- **Comedy rule:** Tweedsie *rarely starts* conflict. He humiliates pompous power and exits before anyone can explain his trick.
- **Signature attitude:** “Same hole. Different day.”
- **Game mechanic:** spotting optional appearances can unlock harmless Easter eggs or Golden Stems; make the jokes surprising, never punish players who miss him.
- **Music:** a specially composed original retro-rock cue, sometimes from Lenny Smokins.

### Lenny Smokins [LOCKED concept]
- In-universe retro-rock star, outrageous music-video energy and an impressive mustache.
- Tentative original song title: **“I'm Real High”**.

- Musical direction may be broadly 1980s triumphant rock, but use **new melodies, original lyrics, original recording and original artwork**. Do not borrow any portion of an existing song.

## TweedlePD supporting cast

### Winston "Winnie" Kodak III [LOCKED concept]
- **Everyone at TVPD calls him Winnie.** His formal name is Winston Kodak III.
- TVPD photographer who materializes at the least appropriate moments, photographs people mid-scene, then offers absurdly inappropriate print options.
- The precinct contains a **secret room the department forgot about**, packed with **miles of hanging photo negatives**.
- The committed name, nickname, role, recurring gag, and forgotten negative-filled room are creator-established. Detailed room layout, camera collection, archive mysteries, and side-quest beats are **proposals**, not shipped gameplay.
- His humor is the collision of meticulous service and chaotic emergencies; he treats every picture like an ordinary customer order.
- Full character sheet, original dialogue and future optional quest: [Winnie Kodak III — The Forgotten Darkroom](WINNIE_KODAK_III.md).

### Merv [LOCKED concept]
- Stranded visitor and reconnaissance operative **from another world**, connected to Winnie and the forgotten precinct darkroom.
- Arrived in a small **instant-camera-shaped spacecraft**, **The Last Exposure**, on a **one-way mission**.
- His mission was to send **one photograph** home containing all his gathered intelligence; he has **run out of blank photographs**.
- Special photos can act as **portals**; ordinary precinct photos do not automatically do so.
- Exact anatomy, homeworld, mission target, final-sheet solution, portal constraints, and return prospects remain **open design proposals**.
- His developing friendship with Winnie provides a sincere counterweight to the department's absurd comedy.
- Full premise and potential arc: [Merv and The Last Exposure](MERV_THE_LAST_EXPOSURE.md).

## Voice / writing guardrails

Give each character a distinctive thinking rhythm rather than an impression of a famous comic. Each joke should still make sense with all borrowed references removed. Avoid repetitive “bro” lines in every character's mouth. An earned silence, look at camera, or misplaced receipt is often funnier than a long punchline.

## Continuity template

For each major character eventually maintain: stable design sheet, relationships, motivation, fear, speech patterns, props, episode appearances, knowledge of the fourth wall, and evolving running jokes. No AI-generated improvisation output automatically becomes canon until reviewed.
